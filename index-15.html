The rules that make it click

Five rules

RULE 1 · COUNT

31 nerve pairs, 33 vertebrae

  • 8 cervical nerves, but only 7 cervical vertebrae
  • 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral (fused into the sacrum)
  • 1 coccygeal nerve; about 4 fused coccygeal vertebrae
RULE 2 · WHERE NERVES EXIT

C8 is the switch

C1–C7 leave above the vertebra with the same number (C6 exits between C5 and C6). C8 has no vertebra, so it takes the C7–T1 gap. From T1 down, every nerve leaves below its vertebra (L4 exits between L4 and L5).

RULE 3 · THE CORD IS SHORTER

Segments sit higher than their vertebra

The column grows faster than the cord, so the adult cord ends at L1–L2 (around L3 in a newborn). Rough offsets:

  • Cervical vertebra: add 1 (C5 vertebra → C6 segment)
  • T1–T6: add 2 · T7–T9: add 3
  • T10 → L1–L2 · T11 → L3–L4 · T12 → L5
  • L1 vertebra → all sacral + Co1 (conus)
RULE 4 · DISC HERNIATION

Name the lower number

A typical posterolateral lumbar disc hits the root that exits one level lower: L4–L5 disc → L5 root; L5–S1 → S1. In the neck the same disc hits the root of the lower vertebra because roots exit above: C5–C6 disc → C6.

RULE 5 · WHAT EACH REGION FEEDS

Plexuses and autonomic outflow

  • Cervical plexus C1–C4 · phrenic C3–C5
  • Brachial plexus C5–T1 (cervical enlargement)
  • Intercostals T1–T11, subcostal T12
  • Lumbar plexus L1–L4 · sacral plexus L4–S4
  • Sympathetic T1–L2/L3 · parasympathetic S2–S4

Layers around and inside the cord

From the bone inward

  1. Vertebra (body, pedicles, laminae) and ligamentum flavum
  2. Epidural space — fat and the internal venous plexus; epidural anesthesia goes here
  3. Dura mater — tough outer sac, ends at S2
  4. Subdural space — a potential space only
  5. Arachnoid mater
  6. Subarachnoid space — CSF; the target of a lumbar puncture
  7. Pia mater — on the cord itself; forms the denticulate ligaments and the filum terminale

Inside the cord

  1. White matter outside — ascending and descending tracts
  2. Dorsal horn — sensory input (dorsal roots, dorsal root ganglia)
  3. Lateral horn — only T1–L2/L3: sympathetic preganglionic neurons (intermediolateral cell column)
  4. Ventral horn — motor neurons to skeletal muscle (ventral roots)
  5. Central canal in the middle

Grey matter is biggest in the cervical and lumbosacral enlargements, where the limbs are supplied.
